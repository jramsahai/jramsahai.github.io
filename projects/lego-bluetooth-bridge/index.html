LEGO Bluetooth Bridge

An ESP32 bridge that drives a LEGO Powered Up car over Bluetooth from any input device you choose

  • Personal
  • Builder
  • 2026
  • Source on GitHub

The problem

LEGO's Powered Up motorized sets, including the Technic 42160 Audi RS Q e-tron this was built against, only respond to LEGO's own CONTROL+ phone app. This works as a simple touchscreen remote, however I wanted a more refined controller, and there's no official way to steer it with anything else: a physical controller, a different sensor, or your own code.

The solution

I built an ESP32-based controller that acts as a Bluetooth Low Energy client speaking LEGO Wireless Protocol 3.0, the same protocol the official app uses to talk to the hub. It connects, calibrates the steering motor to its end stops on every connection, and turns simple text commands arriving over a serial line into motor output. Because it's just another BLE client on the same protocol, nothing on the car is modified: switching back to the CONTROL+ app only requires a power cycle of the car.

The system is three small, independently testable pieces joined by two plain interfaces: an ASCII serial protocol in, BLE out. The ESP32 bridge is the reusable core and is agnostic about what produced the frames it receives. The reference controller is a BBC micro:bit that reads its accelerometer and turns tilt into steer and throttle values, but it's just one example. Any device that can emit the same serial frames, like a game controller, a laptop, or another microcontroller, is a drop-in replacement. A third piece, a small Node.js harness on the Mac, talks to the hub directly for port discovery and calibration testing, independent of the embedded code on the microcontrollers.

Two design choices came out of building this the hard way. First, the car boots disarmed: the ESP32 starts with its pairing mode enabled and only sets it on a deliberate signal after calibration completes, so power-cycling on a table or a brief signal dropout mid-drive can never move the car on its own. Second, the status the ESP32 reports back travels as an ASCII digit rather than a raw byte, because a raw control byte happened to collide with the micro:bit's own MicroPython console and would silently kill the controller script.

Tech stack

An embedded BLE central talking to an embedded sensor node over a plain serial protocol, with a separate desktop harness for developing and debugging against the hub directly.
